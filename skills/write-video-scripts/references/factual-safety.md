# Factual, Rights, and Safety Guidance

Load this reference when a script makes consequential claims, uses real people, adapts protected material, or depicts risky conduct.

## Classify the claim risk

Treat these as verification-sensitive:

- Current events, schedules, prices, rankings, platform rules, or public-office facts
- Statistics, research findings, quotations, historical specifics, and superlatives
- Product features, performance, availability, comparisons, and testimonials
- Medical, legal, financial, employment, housing, or safety guidance
- Allegations, criminal conduct, reputation-sensitive statements, and personal data
- A real person's words, endorsement, consent, identity, or likeness

Creative details in clearly fictional work do not require sourcing, but do not attach invented conduct or quotations to a real person in a misleading way.

## Verify responsibly

1. Prefer primary and authoritative sources.
2. Confirm that a source directly supports the exact wording, population, date, and scope of the claim.
3. Use a second independent source when a claim is contested, consequential, or easy to misinterpret.
4. Record source links and the supported claim in verification notes.
5. Use cautious language only when it remains accurate; do not use hedging to smuggle in an unsupported assertion.
6. If verification is unavailable, omit the detail or label it `[VERIFY]` for the production team.

Never fabricate a URL, citation, quote, study, award, credential, testimonial, clinical result, or product capability.

## Handle supplied material

- Treat a user-provided claim as source material, not automatically as verified fact.
- Preserve approved brand language only when it does not create deception or an unsafe guarantee.
- Separate what the source states from what can be independently confirmed.
- Flag contradictions instead of silently choosing the more convenient version.

## Protect people and audiences

- Do not create a false real-person endorsement, impersonation, confession, or deceptive synthetic-media premise.
- Frame reenactments and dramatizations so an audience can recognize them as such when confusion is plausible.
- Remove unnecessary personal or sensitive information.
- Do not turn rumors or unverified accusations into factual narration.
- Apply extra care to minors, intimate content, vulnerable people, and power imbalances.

## Handle high-stakes and dangerous topics

- Avoid personalized medical, legal, or financial conclusions and guaranteed outcomes.
- Avoid step-by-step operational detail that meaningfully enables violence, abuse, evasion, self-harm, or illegal wrongdoing.
- Redirect unsafe concepts toward prevention, recovery, news reporting, historical context, or clearly non-operational fiction when possible.
- Make required warnings specific and brief; do not let a disclaimer excuse a misleading core claim.

## Respect copyright and style

- Do not reproduce long passages from copyrighted scripts, books, lyrics, or articles unless the user has supplied and authorized them for transformation within applicable limits.
- Summarize or transform source material and preserve attribution where required.
- Do not promise an exact imitation of a living creator's signature style. Translate the request into non-exclusive traits such as sentence length, pacing, warmth, visual density, humor type, or narrative structure.
- Do not include third-party footage, music, images, fonts, or trademarks in production notes as if their rights were cleared.

## Write transparent verification notes

Use this compact pattern:

```markdown
- **Claim:** What the script says
  - **Status:** Verified / User-supplied / Creative dramatization / Needs verification
  - **Source or action:** Link, supplied document, or required production check
```
