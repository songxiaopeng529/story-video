# Quality Rubric

Run this rubric silently before delivery. Revise any hard-gate failure.

## Hard gates

Confirm every item:

- Cover every explicit hard constraint from the brief.
- Keep the estimated timeline and spoken volume plausible for the target duration.
- Establish a clear viewing reason, question, or situation early.
- Maintain one controlling idea and a causally coherent progression.
- Make narration and dialogue natural to say aloud.
- Keep cast, shots, locations, props, and assets executable within stated limits.
- Give visuals and audio a purpose beyond duplicating narration.
- Preserve source meaning and distinguish fact, inference, dramatization, and verification gaps.
- Avoid invented figures, quotes, endorsements, product behavior, or credentials.
- Align any CTA with the stated objective and avoid adding one mechanically.
- Match the requested language, tone, format, and level of detail.

## Score the draft

Use a 1–5 internal score for each dimension:

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brief fit | Misses core constraints | Covers the request with minor drift | Every choice serves the brief |
| Hook / setup | Generic or confusing | Clear but familiar | Specific, relevant, and earned |
| Structure | List-like or disjointed | Understandable progression | Each beat creates the next |
| Speakability | Written prose | Mostly natural | Effortless aloud with intentional rhythm |
| Visual execution | Decorative or vague | Shootable | Visuals carry meaning and pacing |
| Timing | Implausible | Roughly workable | Beat and read-through estimates align |
| Factual trust | Unsupported claims | Gaps are marked | Claims are sourced, bounded, or omitted |
| Payoff / CTA | Missing or bolted on | Functional | Resolves the promise and motivates naturally |

Target at least 4 in brief fit, structure, speakability, timing, visual execution, and factual trust. Do not compensate for a hard-gate failure with a higher average.

## Tighten the final draft

1. Cut greetings and setup the viewer does not need.
2. Replace abstractions with a visible action, example, object, consequence, or line of dialogue.
3. Remove repeated information across narration, visuals, and on-screen text.
4. Shorten any sentence that needs a second breath without deliberate dramatic purpose.
5. Check names, numbers, chronology, prop continuity, and shot-to-shot state.
6. Confirm that the final beat pays off the opening promise.
7. Return only material assumptions and open questions that affect production or truthfulness.
